    Manager's Handbook

    Quick definitions — keep this open while you trade.

    Revenue
    The total money coming in from sales: price × units sold. Not the same as profit.
    Fixed costs
    Costs that stay the same no matter how much you sell or make, e.g. stall hire, permits, equipment rental for the day.
    Variable costs
    Costs that rise and fall with how many units you make, e.g. ingredients, packaging — paid on every unit you prepare, sold or not.
    Contribution margin
    Selling price minus variable cost per unit. It's what each sale actually contributes toward covering your fixed costs.
    Break-even point
    The number of units you must sell so total revenue exactly equals total costs — profit is zero. Formula: fixed costs ÷ contribution margin.
    Margin of safety
    How far actual sales are above (or below) the break-even point. A bigger margin means the business can absorb a bad day and stay viable.
    Forecast vs actual
    Your forecast is an educated guess at demand before trading; actual sales can differ once real conditions (weather, competitors, hype) play out.
    Profit ≠ viability
    One profitable day doesn't prove a business is viable. Viability means it can consistently cover its costs, absorb shocks, and keep enough cash to keep operating — not just get lucky once.
